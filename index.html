Welcome!

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Economics at the University of Washington.

My research is in applied microeconomics, with a focus on public and labor economics. I study how firms and households respond to government policies and economic shocks, and how these responses shape labor markets. I use modern causal inference tools such as difference-in-differences and synthetic methods in conjunction with machine learning to study these questions.

My job market paper examines how a change to state tax code impacts employment and firm formation. More broadly, my work leverages large-scale survey and administrative data to measure the heterogeneous effects of public programs.

Before starting a Ph.D. at UW, I completed a bachelor's degree in Economics with minor focuses in Mathematics and Chinese at Michigan State University. In my spare time, I enjoy reading, baking, and cooking.

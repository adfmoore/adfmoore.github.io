Passing the Buck: The Effects of a Targeted Tax Cut for Pass-Through Businesses

What are the effects of tax cuts for business owners on employment? Exploiting the elimination of Kansas state taxes on pass-through income, this paper estimates the labor market effects of a large and sudden reduction in taxes on business owners. I find that the reform reduced total employment by 2.3%, with larger declines concentrated outside its intended beneficiaries. Pass-through businesses experienced modest employment losses but no decline in payroll, resulting in higher average earnings among retained workers. Non-profit organizations, by contrast, experienced the largest and most persistent employment declines, consistent with a reduction in the relative tax advantage of non-profit status. Evidence from consumption data suggests the reform acted as a negative demand shock, with tax savings flowing into savings rather than spending. Together, these results highlight that targeted tax cuts can have unintended consequences for non-beneficiary organizations and underscore the importance of demand-side responses in evaluating tax policy.
